On the chilly late-November mornings, the Son Tra peninsula has become a meeting point for crowds of locals and visitors, mostly young people, who come to walk, run, trek and hike. On the morning of 24 November, the surge in people and vehicles caused traffic jams at times around the Yet Kieu checkpoint, and the road up to Ban Co Peak was lined with a long trail of backpackers in the mist.

The Management Board of Son Tra Peninsula and Da Nang’s Tourist Beaches has issued a set of advisories. DanangStay sums up what you need to remember, along with a few tips from locals.

Traffic and parking advice

  • Park only in the designated areas, line your vehicle up neatly and keep it off the roadway.
  • Do not stop or park at random along either side of the road or block the entrance to the checkpoint.
  • Follow the instructions of checkpoint staff; if a car park is full, move your vehicle elsewhere.
  • Tour vehicles and coaches with 16 or more seats may stop at the checkpoint only to drop off and pick up passengers, then must head back down the mountain rather than wait at the checkpoint.

Health advice for trekking and hiking

  • Eat properly and don’t climb on an empty stomach; bring water and light snacks for energy.
  • Choose a route and pace within your ability, and rest as soon as you feel tired.
  • If you feel dizzy, have blurred vision, break into a sweat or your hands and legs start shaking, stop and ask for help.
  • If someone faints or has a seizure, call 115 for an ambulance or alert Management Board staff. Never give food or drink to anyone who is unconscious or having a seizure.
  • Stay with your group, don’t wander off or enter restricted areas, keep the area clean, and do not feed the wildlife, especially the red-shanked douc langurs.

Tips for travellers

  • Go very early or avoid weekends. Roughly 5:30 to 7 am is the busiest time; if you just want the views, go on a weekday or after 8:30 am when it is quieter.
  • Steep roads with tight bends. Motorbikes need good brakes and shouldn’t be overloaded; in a small car, use a low gear on the way down. Roads get slippery in the rain, so postpone if it is wet.
  • Bring a light jacket. At this time of year, Ban Co Peak is noticeably colder and mistier in the early morning than the city below.
  • Check the weather the day before. On days of heavy rain, the Management Board usually advises limiting outdoor activities on the peninsula.
  • Combine it with sightseeing: Linh Ung Pagoda at Bai But, the thousand-year-old banyan tree and Bai But beach, then down to Man Thai for seafood make a perfect morning.
