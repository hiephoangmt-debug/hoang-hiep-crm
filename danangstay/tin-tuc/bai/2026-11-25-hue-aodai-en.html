On the morning of 24 November, Vietnam Culture Day, around 200 locals and visitors dressed in the traditional ao dai and cycled together through the streets of Hue. The sight of the ao dai riders gliding past the Citadel and across two bridges over the Perfume River made for a very "Hue" morning, and it also suggests a gentle way to explore the old imperial capital that travellers can easily try for themselves.

The ao dai bicycle parade in Hue

Participants gathered early in the morning at Ngo Mon Square (in front of the Hue Imperial City). The event was organised by the Hue Monuments Conservation Centre, which had around 200 bicycles ready for the group.

Starting from Ngo Mon Square, the route passed through:

  1. Le Huan, Ong Ich Khiem and Cua Nha Do streets
  2. Le Duan street, then over Nguyen Hoang Bridge across the Perfume River
  3. Bui Thi Xuan and Le Loi streets
  4. Over Phu Xuan Bridge, back to Cua Ngan and the starting point

The ride took in many streets and signature landscapes, showing off the old capital's architecture, scenery and everyday city life to locals and visitors alike.

Why bicycles and ao dai?

According to the Hue Monuments Conservation Centre, bicycles were chosen to support the trend towards green, eco-friendly transport, and to encourage people to stay fit and live actively.

Holding the event on Vietnam Culture Day, 24 November, also carried a message of spreading love for heritage and encouraging the community, especially young people, to take an active part in preserving and promoting traditional culture. The organisers hope the activity will bring the community closer to its heritage and promote Hue as a heritage, cultural, ecological, scenic and environmentally friendly city.

Tips for travellers

The tips below are first-hand advice compiled by DanangStay and are not part of the original news report.

  • Cycle a similar route yourself. Looping around Ngo Mon, following the Citadel gates and then crossing a bridge to the south bank of the Perfume River is a wonderfully relaxed way to see Hue. Many hotels and shops around the centre rent bicycles by the hour or by the day.
  • Go early in the morning or late in the afternoon. The streets are quieter, the sun is softer and the light is better for ao dai photos.
  • Ride safely in an ao dai. Tie up the back panel or drape it to the front so it doesn't catch in the wheel or chain; wear trousers that aren't too wide and flat shoes.
  • Watch the end-of-year weather. November is the rainy season in Hue, so bring a light raincoat and check the forecast before you set off.
  • Rent an ao dai for photos. There are plenty of places around the Imperial City that rent out ao dai and traditional headwraps (khan van); ask about the price and return time before you rent.
  • Coming from Da Nang. Hue is doable as a day trip from Da Nang, but if you want to ride early in the morning, it's best to stay a night in Hue.
