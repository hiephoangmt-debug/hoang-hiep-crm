For the early-2027 travel season, Da Nang’s coastline is getting a floral makeover. The Da Nang City People’s Committee has just approved the investment plan for the “Seaside Flower Road” project, featuring 21 installations that combine fresh flowers with artistic lighting, stretching from the central coastal roads to the well-known beaches of An Bang, Cua Dai and Tam Thanh. Here are the key points to help you plan a photo stop.

What is the “Seaside Flower Road” project?

  • Total investment of VND 16.175 billion from the city budget, carried out over two years, 2026–2027.
  • 21 installations of floral decoration and lighting, arranged as a continuous landscape along Vo Nguyen Giap, Hoang Sa and Nguyen Tat Thanh streets and at An Bang, Cua Dai and Tam Thanh beaches.
  • Themes revolve around the sea, nature, culture and tourism, mixing art installations, flower carpets and light effects.
  • Materials include steel, concrete, wood and composite.
  • The Management Board of Son Tra Peninsula and Da Nang’s Tourist Beaches is in charge of managing and delivering the project.

The city’s aim is to create a highlight for the coastal cityscape, brighten up public spaces, give residents and visitors somewhere to stroll and enjoy, and help promote Da Nang’s image.

On display: January to the end of April 2027

According to the plan, the flower clusters and installations will stay in place for 4 months once completed, from January 2027 to the end of April 2027. This early part of the year is packed with travel and leisure activities, so the flower road should be a standout outdoor experience for locals and visitors from further afield alike.

Photo-worthy installations, street by street

Vo Nguyen Giap Street has the largest concentration of sea-themed installations:

  • The “Da Nang Seaside Flower Road Welcome Gate” at the Lang Ong area, at the Vo Nguyen Giap – Vo Van Kiet junction.
  • The installations “Beach Morning Glory”, “Octopus Family”, “Jellyfish”, “Sails Reaching Far”, “Pisces” and “Flower Boat”.
  • A “Flower Tower” paired with DANANG letters at the Vo Nguyen Giap – Nguyen Van Thoai junction.
  • “Hoi An Lanterns” and “Soul of Thanh Ha Pottery”, bringing the culture of the heritage region to the beachfront.
  • Some installations from previous years are being reused and refurbished, with smaller pieces dotted along the route to keep the landscape flowing.

Hoang Sa Street has “School of Fish” at the Hoang Sa – Le Duc Tho junction and “Surfing Boat” at the Hoang Sa – Nguyen Huy Chuong junction.

Nguyen Tat Thanh Street features “Whale Reaching for the Open Sea” at Nguyen An Ninh Seaside Park, together with flower carpets and artistic lights.

The southern beaches: An Bang beach (Hoi An Tay ward) is set to get a name-sign cluster with flower carpets and lights; Cua Dai beach (Hoi An Dong ward) will have three clownfish made from reclaimed wood; and Tam Thanh beach (Quang Phu ward) will have an art installation of wooden boats.

Tips for travellers

  • Walk the length of Vo Nguyen Giap. Most installations are on this road, so start at the welcome gate at Lang Ong and work your way south. If you are on a motorbike, use the beachside car parks and don’t pull over at the kerb to take photos.
  • Pick your time. Early morning is cool, with soft light and fewer people; late afternoon into evening is when the decorative lights come on and photos take on a completely different mood.
  • Early-year weather. January–February in Da Nang can still be windy with drizzle, so bring a light jacket; March–April brings fine weather and gentle sunshine, perfect for beach walks.
  • Combine it with Hoi An. If you are staying in Hoi An, visit An Bang and Cua Dai in the morning and head to the Ancient Town in the afternoon; Tam Thanh is further away, so plan a separate day there together with its mural village.
  • Look after the installations. Don’t climb on them, pick the flowers or leave litter, so the flower road stays beautiful until the end of the season.
