The Da Nang City People's Committee has just approved a master plan for eco-tourism, resort and recreation development in the Cu Lao Cham Nature Reserve up to 2030, with a vision to 2050. Under the plan, the well-known island group off the coast of Hoi An will have 18 sites for tourism activities and 12 eco-tourism routes, with one guiding principle throughout: nature conservation comes first.

Visitor targets for 2030 and 2050

  • By 2030, Cu Lao Cham aims to be able to welcome around 350,000 visitors a year: 140,000 international and 210,000 domestic.
  • Of these, about 35,000 visitors are expected to stay overnight on the island.
  • Total tourism revenue is estimated at around VND 350 billion a year; the plan also aims to attract 3–5 investors and partners and create jobs for about 400 local workers.
  • Vision to 2050: serving and managing around 400,000 visitors a year, with expected revenue of VND 600 billion a year, but only on the basis of controlling the ecosystem's carrying capacity.
  • The indicative budget for implementing the plan up to 2030 is about VND 346.5 billion.

18 sites and 12 eco-tourism routes

The 18 eco-tourism, resort and recreation sites cover a total area of almost 510 hectares. On land, the forest has 12 eco-experience sites covering more than 257 hectares; at sea there are 6 sites within an eco-tourism zone of more than 252 hectares.

Alongside these are 12 eco-tourism routes with a combined length of 39.8 km. The routes make use of waterways, roads, forest patrol tracks and existing footpaths on the island.

Conservation is the top priority

The most notable part of the plan is its commitment not to trade away the forest, coral reef and seagrass ecosystems for tourism. Specifically:

  • No resort or recreation activities are allowed in the strictly protected zone.
  • In the ecological restoration zone, tourism facilities may occupy no more than 2% of the area used for tourism activities; in the service and administrative zone the limit is 5%.
  • At sea, no permanent structures may be built on the seabed, and the natural underwater environment must be left as it is. Eco-moorings and floating pontoons must be demountable to limit their impact.

Cu Lao Cham today

Cu Lao Cham belongs to Tan Hiep island commune. It is made up of several small islands, but only one is inhabited, with about 2,500 residents. It takes around 20 minutes by speedboat from the Hoi An mainland. The islands are part of a biosphere reserve listed by UNESCO and have long been known worldwide for their conservation story. The island currently receives an average of 2,000–3,000 visitors a day.

Tips for travellers

  • Pick the right season. Speedboats to the island run most reliably in the calm-sea months, usually from spring to late summer. The end of the year is the rainy and storm season in central Vietnam, and boats may be suspended when the sea is rough, so always double-check the schedule a day ahead.
  • Go early to beat the crowds. Day tours usually head out in the morning; for quieter beaches, stay a night at a homestay on the island.
  • Respect the conservation rules. Don't step on or break coral, don't take marine life home, cut down on single-use plastic and bring your rubbish back to the mainland. Cu Lao Cham is well known for its efforts to limit plastic bags.
  • Snorkel with a guide. Go with an operator that provides life jackets and a guide, and only snorkel in permitted areas.
  • Pack the essentials: cash, a hat, sunscreen, seasickness tablets and a waterproof pouch for your phone.
