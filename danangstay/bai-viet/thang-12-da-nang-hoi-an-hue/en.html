Plenty of people write off December as the “wrong season” for central Vietnam because of the rain. Locals in Da Nang know better: this is when the city is at its most pleasant for slow travel. The summer heat has eased, the northeast wind brings a cool breath off the sea, and the streets start lighting up for Christmas and the New Year. Hoi An is at its loveliest on chilly late afternoons, when kitchen smoke drifts through the lantern light. Hue wears a fine veil of drizzle — that unmistakable Hue mood some travellers wait a lifetime to see.

Written by locals, this guide brings together everything you need for a three-stop trip to Da Nang, Hoi An and Hue in December 2026: realistic weather, the festival calendar, the best things to see in each place, warming dishes for cool days, what to do when it rains, and three sample itineraries by trip length.

December at a glance

  • Weather: cool, around 19–25°C in the lowlands; Ba Na Hills and Hue are colder. Rainy spells of 2–4 days still happen, with plenty of dry days in between.
  • Sea: usually rough because of the northeast monsoon — more for watching than swimming.
  • Dates to remember: Hoi An International Lantern Festival 3–6 December, Da Nang Christmas Market 4–6 December, Hoi An Old Town lantern night on 22 December (14th day of the 11th lunar month), Christmas on 24–25 December, and the 2027 New Year festivities from 30 December.
  • New Year holiday: 1 January 2027 falls on a Friday, so many Vietnamese get a three-day break (1–3 January). Hotels along the Han River and the beach will be full — book a few weeks ahead.
  • Prices: outside New Year’s Eve, room rates are usually softer than in summer and sights are less crowded.

Should you visit Da Nang, Hoi An and Hue in December?

Go if you like cool weather, want photos of the Old Town without the crowds, care more about culture and food than beach time, or want to see in the New Year in a seaside city with fireworks, music and a festive buzz.

Think twice if your main goals are swimming, snorkelling the coral at Cu Lao Cham (Cham Islands) or photographing the Golden Bridge in sunshine — all of these depend heavily on the weather. Boats to Cu Lao Cham are often restricted or suspended when the sea is rough, so don’t build your whole trip around them.

A local’s secret: don’t lock your plans to fixed dates. Keep a “sunny plan” and a “rainy plan” for each day, and decide the night before after checking the forecast.

Can you swim in the sea in December?

You can, but not every day is safe. The northeast monsoon often brings high waves and strong offshore currents at My Khe, Man Thai and An Bang beaches. Simple rules: only go in on calm mornings, stay in areas with a lifeguard tower, and never swim when the red flag is up. On rough days the sea is beautiful in a different way — white-capped waves, silver-grey skies, perfect for an early run or photos. Travelling with young children? Choose a hotel with a heated pool.

Local tip

December is a great fish season at Man Thai. Get up around 5–6 am and head to the fishing-village beach to watch the round basket boats come in, with the fish market set up right on the sand. Then have breakfast the Da Nang way: a steaming bowl of bun cha ca (fish cake noodle soup).

Top 5 things to do in Da Nang in December

1. Son Tra Peninsula and Linh Ung Pagoda

Son Tra looks its best on a morning after rain: deep green forest, clouds draped across the mountainside and a crystal-clear view down over Da Nang Bay. Stop at Linh Ung Pagoda (Bai But), whose Lady Buddha — the tallest statue in the city — faces the sea. The mountain road is steep with many bends; on rainy days go by car rather than motorbike.

2. Man Thai fishing village at sunrise

Basket boats landing, a fish market on the sand, no-frills seafood joints along Hoang Sa street — this is the most “real” Da Nang, just over ten minutes from the centre. In December the rough sea means boats come back irregularly, so go early and don’t mind if the market is quiet that day.

3. Marble Mountains – a rain-proof stop

A cluster of limestone hills with caves such as Huyen Khong Cave and Tam Thai Pagoda, plus the Non Nuoc stone-carving village at the foot. Most of the sights are covered or inside caves, so you can still visit in light rain; just wear shoes with good grip, as the stone steps get slippery.

4. The Han River at night and the Dragon Bridge

Stroll the east or west bank of the Han River when the bridges light up. The Dragon Bridge breathes fire and water at 21:00 on Saturday and Sunday evenings and on some holidays. Over Christmas and New Year, Bach Dang street is dressed in lights and becomes the liveliest spot in the city.

5. Ba Na Hills – only on a clear day

If the forecast shows a sunny day, head up to Ba Na very early to catch the Golden Bridge before the clouds roll in. The summit is a lot colder than the city, so bring a jacket. If the fog is thick, change the day or swap in Marble Mountains and a museum — don’t waste a cable-car ticket on a day of nothing but mist. Top 5 things to do in Hoi An in December

1. The Old Town in the late afternoon

Arrive around 3–4 pm, once the day-trip groups have thinned out. Wander past the Japanese Covered Bridge (Chua Cau), the assembly halls and old houses in the soft light, then stay on until the lanterns come on. Entering the Old Town requires a sightseeing ticket from an official booth — prices can change, so check when you arrive.

2. Lantern night on 22 December and the early-month Lantern Festival

December 2026 has two memorable lantern occasions: the International Lantern Festival (3–6 December) and the Old Town lantern night on the 14th lunar day (22 December). Releasing a floating flower lantern on the Hoai River is a “very touristy” thing to do — and still surprisingly moving.

3. Make your own lantern

A lantern-making class takes about one to two hours: bending the bamboo frame, stretching the silk, gluing the trim. It’s a souvenir that folds flat in your suitcase — and an ideal rainy-day activity.

4. Cycle to Tra Que vegetable village or Thanh Ha pottery village

On dry days, cycle through the fields to Tra Que vegetable village to learn some gardening and cook local dishes, or stop by Thanh Ha pottery village to try throwing a pot. In December the rice fields are lush green after the rains, and the soft light is lovely for photos.

5. My Son Sanctuary

A complex of Cham temples recognised by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site, about an hour’s drive from Hoi An. If you’re there on 15 December, time your visit for an “Echoes of My Son” show. Go early in the morning to avoid afternoon rain and get beautiful light on the brick walls.

Top 5 things to do in Hue in December

1. The Imperial City in drizzle

Hue’s Imperial City has a quiet, contemplative beauty in the drizzle: green moss on the citadel walls, wet roof tiles, long empty corridors. Go in the morning with an umbrella and grippy shoes. The Imperial City sometimes opens in the evening — if your dates match, don’t miss it.

2. The tombs of Tu Duc, Khai Dinh and Minh Mang

Each tomb has its own character: Tu Duc is poetic, with lotus ponds and pine forest; Khai Dinh is ornate, with mosaics of broken porcelain; Minh Mang is grand and symmetrical. One or two tombs is plenty for half a day; go by car or taxi, as they are far apart.

3. Thien Mu Pagoda and the Perfume River

An ancient pagoda on the bank of the Perfume River (Song Huong), with the seven-storey Phuoc Duyen tower. In the afternoon you can take a dragon boat along the river and watch both banks fade into the mist.

4. Ca Hue folk music on the Perfume River

In the evening, sit on a boat in the middle of the river and listen to ca Hue — court-style music, floating lanterns, sweet and melancholy voices. A quintessential Hue experience, especially in the year-end chill.

5. A day of eating in Hue

Bun bo Hue in the morning, com hen (baby clam rice) and banh beo – nam – loc rice cakes for lunch, che (sweet soups) in the afternoon, then Dong Ba Market and Truong Tien Bridge in the evening. The cold makes every hot dish taste even better.

Local tip

To get from Da Nang to Hue, try the train: the line hugs the coast over the Hai Van Pass and is one of the most beautiful stretches of railway in Vietnam. By car, you’ll go through the Hai Van Tunnel; on rainy days don’t ride a motorbike over the pass, as it gets foggy and slippery. We can arrange private cars and other travel services for the trip.

Top 5 dishes for cool weather

  1. Da Nang bun cha ca – a sweet fish broth with fragrant fried fish cakes; the perfect breakfast on a chilly morning.
  2. Mi Quang – yellow noodles in just a little broth, topped with crumbled grilled rice crackers and fresh herbs.
  3. Bun bo Hue – spicy and fragrant with lemongrass; eaten hot in the drizzle, it’s exactly right.
  4. Banh xeo and nem lui – crispy pancakes and lemongrass skewers eaten hot, dipped in the central region’s signature peanut-liver sauce (nuoc leo).
  5. Grilled seafood at Man Thai – squid, snails and fish grilled over charcoal; ask the price per kilo before you order.

Top 5 things to do on a rainy day

  1. Museum of Cham Sculpture (Da Nang) – one of the finest collections of Cham sculpture anywhere; easily worth half a day.
  2. A cooking or lantern-making class in Hoi An – learn something and take home a gift.
  3. Rain-watching over coffee – a salt coffee or egg coffee by a window looking out on the Han River or the Old Town.
  4. Han Market and Con Market – covered, full of snacks and local specialities to take home.
  5. Spa and massage – after a day on your feet, the best way to warm up.

Suggested itineraries by trip length

3 days, 2 nights: Da Nang – Hoi An

  • Day 1: Son Tra and Linh Ung Pagoda; seafood lunch at Man Thai; evening on the Han River and the Dragon Bridge.
  • Day 2: Morning at Marble Mountains; afternoon to Hoi An, exploring the Old Town into the evening; overnight in Hoi An.
  • Day 3: Morning at Tra Que vegetable village or a lantern-making class; afternoon back to Da Nang and the airport.

4 days, 3 nights: add Ba Na or My Son

  • As the 3-day plan, plus a day at Ba Na (if the sky is clear) or a morning at My Son before heading into the Old Town.

5 days, 4 nights: all three – Da Nang, Hoi An and Hue

  • Days 1–2: Da Nang (Son Tra, Man Thai, Marble Mountains, Han River).
  • Day 3: Hoi An from midday into the evening; overnight in the Old Town.
  • Day 4: Train to Hue; Imperial City in the afternoon, ca Hue on the Perfume River in the evening; overnight in Hue.
  • Day 5: Morning at the Tu Duc or Khai Dinh tomb and Thien Mu Pagoda; afternoon back to Da Nang.

Where to stay in December

In December, pick a convenient base so you can easily switch plans around the weather:

  • My Khe – An Thuong: close to the beach, packed with restaurants and cafés, near everything. Great for a first visit to Da Nang.
  • Man Thai – Son Tra: quieter, right by the fishing village and seafood market, close to the peninsula. Great for families and slow travellers.
  • Han River centre: handy for the fireworks, the countdown and the Dragon Bridge; about 10 minutes from the airport.
  • Hoi An: spend a night in or near the Old Town to enjoy the evening after the day-trippers have gone.

Frequently asked questions

Is December a good time to visit Da Nang?

Yes, if you like cool weather, festive lights, fewer crowds and softer room rates. Keep your plans flexible, as rainy spells lasting a few days still happen.

Is Da Nang cold in December?

Not like northern Vietnam: daytime is around 20–25°C, with cool early mornings and evenings. The Ba Na Hills summit and Hue are colder, so bring a jacket.

When is the Hoi An Old Town lantern night in December 2026?

On 22 December 2026, which is the 14th day of the 11th lunar month. The lantern night takes place on the 14th day of every lunar month.

Can you visit Cu Lao Cham (Cham Islands) in December?

Usually it’s difficult. When the sea is rough, boats to the islands are often restricted or suspended. If you want to go, treat it as an “if you’re lucky” activity and check in advance with the operators at Cua Dai port.

How long does it take to get from Da Nang to Hue?

About 2–3 hours by car through the Hai Van Tunnel, or about 2.5–3 hours by train along the coastal line.

Should I book accommodation in advance for New Year?

Yes. The nights of 30 December – 1 January are peak, and this year 1 January 2027 falls on a Friday, so many people are off until 3 January. Hotels near the Han River and East Sea Park fill up first.
