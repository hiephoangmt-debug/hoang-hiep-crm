Han Market Da Nang (Chợ Hàn) is at 119 Tran Phu Street in the city centre. It sits right by the west bank of the Han River, a few minutes' walk from the Han River Bridge. Stalls generally open from about 6am until early evening, around 7pm, though each vendor sets their own hours. The ground floor sells dried goods, coffee, cashews and local snacks, and has a food area. The upper floor is all fabrics, clothes and souvenirs, with tailors who can make an áo dài in a few hours. For street food at local prices, add Con Market (Chợ Cồn), about 1 km away.

This guide was written by Da Nang locals. It covers what to buy in Da Nang's best-known market, what to eat, how to bargain without overpaying, how to pack your souvenirs for the flight, and whether Han Market or Con Market suits you better.

Where is Han Market and how do you get there?

Han Market is the oldest market in central Da Nang. It took shape under French rule. The current two-storey building was rebuilt around 1989–1991. In 2022 the city spent more than 8.1 billion dong renovating it as a market for visitors, with new wiring and plumbing and a photo spot.

  • Address: 119 Tran Phu, Hai Chau, Da Nang. There are entrances on all four sides: Tran Phu, Bach Dang (the riverside), Hung Vuong and Nguyen Thai Hoc.
  • From My Khe Beach: cross the Han River Bridge or the Dragon Bridge. A Grab takes about 10–15 minutes, depending on traffic.
  • From the airport: about 3 km, roughly 10 minutes by car.

Han Market opening hours and the best time to go

Published hours vary from source to source. Some say about 6am–7pm, others say until 10pm, because each stall keeps its own schedule.

  • Wet market (fish, meat, vegetables): busiest before 8am. It thins out by early afternoon.
  • Dried goods and clothes: easiest to shop from about 8:30–11am or 3–5:30pm. Middays inside the market are hot.
  • Tết (Lunar New Year): many stalls close for several days.

Customs tip: meat products such as beef jerky or chả bò (beef sausage) are banned or restricted by many countries, including Australia, the US, South Korea and EU members. Stick to dried seafood, coffee, nuts and sweets, and check your own country's rules.

Can you really get an áo dài made the same day?

The upper-floor fabric stalls are known for fast tailoring. You choose the fabric, get measured and pick a style, then come back a few hours later. Da Nang's local paper has reported customers collecting theirs in about an hour. For the best fit, order in the morning, collect the same day, and try it on at the stall so they can adjust it.

What to eat at Han Market

  • Mì Quảng: wide turmeric noodles in a little rich broth, with sesame rice crackers and fresh herbs.
  • Bún chả cá: fishcake noodle soup, a Da Nang breakfast favourite.
  • Bánh xèo and nem lụi: crispy pancakes and grilled pork skewers, rolled in rice paper with herbs.
  • Chè: sweet dessert soups, perfect after a hot shopping run.

Expect about 20,000–50,000 dong per dish (for reference). For evening plans, see our guide to Da Nang nightlife and night markets.

Bargaining and avoiding overcharging

  1. Know where to bargain. Clothes, fabric and souvenirs are negotiable. Food stalls and speciality stalls with price tags usually aren't.
  2. Compare two or three stalls before buying anything pricey, such as dried squid or an áo dài.
  3. Check the unit. Ask whether the price is per kg, per 100 g (lạng) or per pack, and ask them to weigh it in front of you.
  4. Keep it friendly. Knocking 10–30% off clothes and souvenirs is common. If the price doesn't work, say "cảm ơn" (thank you) and walk on. Some vendors care a lot about their first sale of the morning, so don't haggle too hard early on.
  5. Check expiry dates on packaged dried goods before you pay.

Payment: bring small notes (10,000–50,000 dong). More and more stalls accept QR bank transfers, but cards are rarely taken. There are ATMs and gold shops that exchange currency around the market.

Han Market vs Con Market

Con Market (Chợ Cồn) is on the corner of Hung Vuong and Ong Ich Khiem (290 Hung Vuong), about 1 km west of Han Market. It's bigger, more local and best known for its snack stalls.

Frequently asked questions

What are Han Market's opening hours?

Roughly 6am to around 7pm daily, though each stall sets its own hours and some close earlier. Mid-morning and late afternoon are the most comfortable times to shop.

What should I buy at Han Market?

Dried squid, cashews, Vietnamese coffee and sesame crackers are the best sellers. Upstairs you can have an áo dài made, or pick up conical hats and rattan bags.

Han Market or Con Market?

Han Market is better for souvenirs and tailoring, and it's next to the river. Con Market is better for cheap street food. They're about 1 km apart.

Can I pay by card?

Rarely. Some stalls accept QR transfers, but small cash notes are the easiest way to pay.

Can I fly home with fish sauce and dried squid?

Vacuum-sealed dried goods are usually fine. Fish sauce and other liquids over 100 ml must go in checked luggage, well wrapped. Check your home country's import rules for meat products.
