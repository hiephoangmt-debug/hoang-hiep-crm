This Hoi An travel guide comes down to a few essentials: the old town is only about 30 km from Da Nang, roughly 45 minutes by car; arrive around 3–4 pm and stay into the evening to see the lanterns come on; buy a Hoi An old town ticket to enter the old houses, assembly halls and the Japanese Covered Bridge (two tiers, 80,000 and 120,000 VND under the early-2026 announcement — indicative prices); the most pleasant months are roughly February to April, while October–November is peak flood season, when riverside streets can go under water. One day covers the old town; two days let you add the villages, An Bang beach and the Bay Mau coconut forest.

This guide was written by locals. One administrative note: since 1 July 2025, after the provincial merger, Hoi An belongs to Da Nang city (the old town now sits in Hoi An, Hoi An Dong and Hoi An Tay wards), so maps and bookings show “Da Nang” instead of “Quang Nam”.

Hoi An old town ticket: price and what's included

Hoi An has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999, and ticket revenue funds conservation. According to the Hoi An Centre for Cultural Heritage Management's early-2026 announcement:

  • Two tiers: 80,000 VND and 120,000 VND (indicative). They differ in how many monuments you can enter; many older pages still describe them as a Vietnamese/foreigner split.
  • Included: the general old-town landscape, either the Japanese Covered Bridge or Quan Cong Temple, plus a choice of old houses, assembly halls, family chapels or museums (number depends on the tier).
  • Where to buy: ticket booths at the old-town entrances; in 2026 Hoi An also added automated ticket kiosks.
  • Monument hours: old houses and halls generally open from morning until late afternoon (around 7–8 am to 5–6 pm, indicative); some close for lunch.

Validity and the list of sites can change, so ask at the booth and keep your ticket on you. For tickets bundled with a transfer, or tickets to the Hoi An Memories show, see our attraction tickets page.

Things to do in Hoi An: 10 experiences worth your time

1. Cross the Japanese Covered Bridge

The roofed bridge, dating from around the 17th century, was restored from late 2022 and reopened to visitors in August 2024. It looks newer than before, but its structure was kept almost intact.

  • Time needed: 15–20 minutes. Best time: before 8 am. Tip: shoot from the riverbank to fit the whole roof in.

2. Phuc Kien Assembly Hall and the Chinese halls

Phuc Kien (Fujian) Assembly Hall on Tran Phu Street is the largest and most ornate; the Cantonese and Trieu Chau halls are close by.

  • Time needed: 20–30 minutes. Best time: morning. Tip: these are places of worship, so dress modestly.

3. Tan Ky Old House and other merchant houses

Tan Ky on Nguyen Thai Hoc Street is a classic narrow “tube house” blending Vietnamese, Chinese and Japanese architecture. A family still lives there.

  • Time needed: 15–20 minutes. Best time: morning. Tip: two or three houses are plenty.

4. Watch the town light up

Around 5:30–6:30 pm, while the sky is still blue, lanterns flicker on along the streets and both banks of the Hoai River. Full-moon nights (the 14th of the lunar month) are especially atmospheric.

  • Time needed: 1–2 hours. Best time: 5:30–8 pm.
  • Tip: read our Hoi An lanterns and full-moon night guide.

5. Take a lantern boat on the Hoai River

Small wooden boats pick up passengers along the bank for a 15–20 minute loop. Commonly quoted prices are about 150,000–200,000 VND per boat, and floating lanterns from a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of VND each (indicative).

  • Time needed: 20–30 minutes. Best time: 6–8 pm; busiest around 7–8:30 pm.
  • Tip: agree on the price and duration before boarding, and wear the life jacket.

6. See the Hoi An Memories show

A large open-air production with hundreds of performers retelling 400 years of Hoi An's trading-port history on Cam Nam island.

  • Time needed: about 1 hour. Best time: evening. Tip: middle-tier seats see the whole stage; arrive 30 minutes early.

7. Basket boats in the Bay Mau coconut forest (Cam Thanh)

About 5 km from the old town, you glide through nipa palms in a round basket boat, watch the spinning show and try crab fishing — great fun for families.

  • Time needed: 1–1.5 hours. Best time: morning or late afternoon.
  • Tip: confirm the price and what's included before you get in; tips are optional.

8. Tra Que vegetable village

A herb-growing village where you can watch farmers at work, try a bit of gardening and join a cooking class. About 15 minutes by bicycle from the old town.

  • Time needed: 1–3 hours. Best time: early morning or late afternoon. Tip: village entry is a few tens of thousands of VND (indicative).

9. Thanh Ha pottery village

A pottery village over 500 years old on the Thu Bon River, where you can shape a small piece yourself. The nearby Thanh Ha Terracotta Park has a separate ticket.

  • Time needed: 1–1.5 hours. Best time: morning. Tip: entry is a few tens of thousands of VND (indicative); kids love the clay.

10. An Bang and Cua Dai beaches

An Bang is about 4–5 km from the old town, with a long sandy beach and plenty of beach restaurants. Cua Dai has suffered serious erosion in places.

  • Time needed: half a day. Best time: early morning or after 3 pm, May–August. Tip: swim only where lifeguards are on duty, never on red-flag days.

Further afield: the Cham Islands (20–30 minutes by speedboat from Cua Dai, calm seas usually March–August), My Son Sanctuary (about 1 hour by car) and VinWonders Nam Hoi An (about 25 km south).

Where to stay in Hoi An

  • In or next to the old town: walk everywhere and enjoy the lanterns. Downsides: crowds, noise and flood risk right by the river.
  • Near An Bang – Cua Dai beach: quiet and family-friendly; 10–15 minutes by car to the old town.
  • Among the rice fields (Cam Thanh, Tra Que): homestays and small villas, 10–20 minutes by bike to town.
  • In Da Nang, with a day trip: when most of your plans are in Da Nang.

Hoi An in 1 day and 2 days

One day (return trip from Da Nang)

  1. 2:00 pm – leave Da Nang, optionally stopping at the Marble Mountains.
  2. 3:30 pm – buy tickets; visit the Covered Bridge, Phuc Kien Hall and an old house.
  3. 5:30 pm – stroll the riverside as the lanterns come on.
  4. 6:30 pm – dinner: cao lau, chicken rice, White Rose.
  5. 7:30 pm – lantern boat, or the Hoi An Memories show.
  6. 9:00 pm – back to Da Nang.

Two days, one night

  1. Day 1: afternoon and evening as above; sleep near the old town.
  2. Day 2, early: walk the empty streets at 6–7 am; mi Quang or banh mi for breakfast.
  3. Day 2, morning: cycle to Tra Que village or the Bay Mau basket boats.
  4. Day 2, afternoon: Thanh Ha pottery village or An Bang beach, then back to Da Nang.

Local tips and etiquette

  • Tailoring: order on day one and allow 1–2 days for fittings; bring reference photos, confirm fabric, total price and deposit, and check the seams before paying in full.
  • Bargaining: gently at markets and souvenir stalls; not needed in fixed-price shops.
  • Photos: many old houses are still homes — ask before photographing people and don't sit on residents' doorsteps.
  • Dress: cover shoulders and knees in assembly halls, pagodas and temples.
  • Getting around: motorbikes are restricted in the old-town core — park and walk.
  • Cash: carry small notes for stalls and boats; many places accept QR payment.

FAQ

How long does it take to get from Da Nang to Hoi An?

About 30 km: 40–60 minutes by car or Grab, around 1–1.5 hours by public bus.

How much is the Hoi An old town ticket?

Under the early-2026 announcement there are two tiers, 80,000 and 120,000 VND, differing in the number of monuments included (indicative). Check at the booth, as rules can change.

What is the best time to visit Hoi An?

Roughly February–April is dry, cool and good for walking. May–August is hot but great for the beach. October–November is peak flood season, so keep plans flexible.

Should I visit Hoi An in October or November?

You can, but riverside streets may flood, as they did several times from late October to mid-November 2025. Watch the forecast and keep your plans flexible.

Is Hoi An now part of Da Nang?

Yes. Since 1 July 2025, after Quang Nam and Da Nang merged, Hoi An belongs to Da Nang city. The old town is now in Hoi An, Hoi An Dong and Hoi An Tay wards.
