Look north from any beach in Da Nang and you will see it: a green mountain rising straight from the sea. The Son Tra peninsula is a nature reserve of rainforest, cliffs and small bays — and one of the few places in the world where you can see the red-shanked douc langur, a monkey so colourful it is nicknamed the queen of primates.

What to see

  • Linh Ung Pagoda & the Lady Buddha — the 67-metre statue of Quan The Am faces the sea; the terraces have the best view of the city.
  • The thousand-year banyan — a vast tree with roots like curtains, a short drive up the mountain road.
  • Ban Co peak — the summit, with a sculpture of two men playing chess and views across to the Hai Van Pass.
  • Bai But and Bai Bac — sheltered beaches and coves for a swim away from the city.
  • The langurs — most often seen in the early morning, high in the trees along the road.

Getting around

The easiest way is with a private car and driver for half a day, or a small guided tour. Roads up the mountain are steep and winding; if you are not an experienced rider, do not rent a motorbike here. There are few shops on the mountain, so bring water and snacks.

Respect the reserve

Do not feed or approach the langurs, keep to the roads and paths, and take your rubbish with you. Parts of the peninsula may close to traffic after storms.

A perfect half day

Leave at 6 am. Look for langurs on the way up, watch the light over the bay from Ban Co, visit Linh Ung as the monks finish morning prayers, swim at Bai But, and be back in town for a late breakfast of bún chả cá.
