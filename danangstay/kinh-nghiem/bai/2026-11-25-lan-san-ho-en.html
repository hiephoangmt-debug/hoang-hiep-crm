Snorkeling in Da Nang comes down to two options. The first is the Son Tra peninsula, a half-day speedboat trip around Hon Sup islet and Bai Nam beach. The second is the Cham Islands off Hoi An, where the reefs are bigger and the water is usually clearer. The best season is roughly March to August, when the sea is calm. From October to December the monsoon makes the sea rough, boats to the Cham Islands are often suspended and most snorkel and dive trips stop.

This guide was written by locals. It covers the reef spots that several sources confirm, how snorkeling compares with scuba and sea walking, typical prices, the conservation rules and safety tips.

Where to snorkel around Da Nang and Hoi An

1. Hon Sup (Son Tra peninsula)

What you see: Hon Sup is a small rocky islet at the foot of the Son Tra peninsula. Travel guides say around 54 coral species live here. Within about 200 m of the islet there are good patches of Son Tra coral, small reef fish and anemones.

Getting there: Join a speedboat tour from the marina on the Son Tra side. Tours usually stop at Hon Sup, Bai Nam and Bai Da.

Level: Easy. You get a life jacket, mask, snorkel and fins, and a guide stays with you in the water.

Note: Only go with a licensed operator that leaves from an official pier. The Son Tra border guard has fined motorboats and inflatable boats for taking tourists snorkeling illegally.

2. Bai Nam – Bai Nghe (Son Tra peninsula)

What you see: Large coral heads in shallow water. On clear days you can see them from the boat.

Getting there: Usually part of the same Son Tra speedboat tour, along with views of the cliffs and forest from the sea.

Level: Easy, but keep your distance because the reef is very shallow.

Note: The reef at Bai Nam is close to shore and easily broken by people standing on it. Float in your life jacket to rest. Never stand on coral.

3. Hon Dai (Cham Islands)

What you see: The most visited site, with a shallow coral garden at about 2–5 m, lots of colourful fish and hard coral.

Getting there: Take a speedboat from Cua Dai port in Hoi An to the islands, about 15–20 minutes. From Bai Lang beach it's about another 15 minutes by boat.

Level: Easy to moderate, depending on the waves.

Note: It gets crowded around midday in summer, so take an early boat.

4. Bai Xep – Bai Chong (Cham Islands)

What you see: Coral close to shore. On clear days you can spot the reef from the beach.

Getting there: Tours often stop at beaches such as Bai Ong and Bai Chong to rest, change and swim before or after snorkeling.

Level: Easy, entering from the shore.

Note: Wear water shoes over the rocks, but once you reach the coral, float and swim. Don't walk on the reef.

5. Bai Bac, Hon Mo and the outer reefs (Cham Islands)

What you see: Reefs that guides describe as healthier and less visited.

Getting there: You need a licensed specialist snorkel or dive trip. Some zones of the marine protected area allow snorkeling only, with a permit.

Level: Moderate to hard. Waves are bigger, so trips only go when the sea is very calm.

Note: This is the best choice for scuba diving.

6. Nam O Reef (Lien Chieu)

What you see: Two rock shelves, Ran Ca and Ran Con, at the end of Nam O fishing village, about 17 km from the city centre. It's mostly rock, seaweed and tide pools at low tide. It is not a main coral snorkeling spot.

Level: Mainly for sightseeing and photos. Only get in the water when the sea is flat and you are with someone.

Even in high season the sea can turn rough for a few days. When the authorities close the sea, every boat stays in port. Don't book the island trip for your last day before a flight.

Conservation rules

  • Don't touch, kick or stand on coral.
  • Don't break, catch or take home coral, shells or marine life. Don't feed the fish.
  • Cut down on plastic. The Cham Islands have run a "say no to plastic bags" campaign since 2009 and ask visitors not to bring single-use plastic bags.
  • Boats drop off and pick up only at designated points. Anchoring on coral reefs and seagrass beds is banned.
  • Only snorkel in permitted zones, with a licensed operator, at both Son Tra and the Cham Islands.

The Cham Islands are planning 18 eco-tourism sites. Read more in our news post on the Cham Islands eco-tourism plan to 2030.

Safety

  • Always wear a life jacket when snorkeling, even if you swim well.
  • Swim with a buddy, stay in the area your guide shows you and don't drift far from the boat.
  • Test your mask and snorkel on the boat before you get in.
  • After scuba, don't fly for about 18–24 hours. If you have a heart, lung or ear condition, or are pregnant, ask a doctor first.
  • Cancel if there is a strong wind or rough sea warning. Don't go just to avoid losing a deposit.

What to bring

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (no oxybenzone or octinoxate).
  • A long-sleeved rash guard, hat and sunglasses.
  • Anti-fog spray for your mask, or your own mask if you have one.
  • A dry bag for your phone, a towel and dry clothes.
  • Water shoes if you enter from rocky shores.

Local tips

  • Go early. Morning boats usually get calmer wind, clearer water and fewer people.
  • Pick a calm day. Check the wind and wave forecast the night before, and keep one or two spare days in your plans.
  • Take motion-sickness tablets about 30 minutes before the speedboat. The ride to the Cham Islands can be bumpy.
  • Eat light. A very full or empty stomach makes seasickness worse.

FAQ

Can I snorkel if I can't swim?

Yes. You wear a life jacket, float at the surface and look down at the reef, with a guide nearby. If you are very nervous in water, ask about sea walking, which doesn't need swimming.

Is Son Tra or the Cham Islands better for snorkeling?

The Cham Islands usually have bigger reefs and clearer water, but it takes a full day. Son Tra is close to the city and takes half a day, which suits travellers short on time or with young children.

Can I snorkel at the Cham Islands in November or December?

Usually not. It's storm and northeast monsoon season, boats are often suspended and most trips stop. The good season is roughly March to August.

How much is the Cham Islands entrance fee?

Since 10 June 2026 it is 70,000 VND per person per visit. Most speedboat tickets and day tours already include it, so check when you book.

Can children snorkel?

Children who are comfortable in water and wear a well-fitting life jacket can snorkel at shallow beaches with an adult right beside them. Scuba has its own age limits, which depend on the dive centre, so ask first.
