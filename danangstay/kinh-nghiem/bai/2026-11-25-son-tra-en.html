The short answer: start early (around 6 – 7 am), ride the coastal Hoang Sa road to Linh Ung Pagoda and the Lady Buddha first, then — if the mountain routes are open — continue on the Yet Kieu – Ban Co peak – Bai Bac loop for the big views and a chance to spot red-shanked doucs, come down before closing time and finish with seafood in Man Thai. The Son Tra Peninsula is only about 10 km from central Da Nang, but in the rainy season its sightseeing routes can close at short notice, so always check before you go.

Look north from any beach in Da Nang and you will see it: a green mountain rising straight from the sea. Son Tra is a protected special-use forest of rainforest, cliffs and small bays — and one of the few places in the world where you can see the red-shanked douc, a monkey so colourful it is nicknamed the queen of primates. Son Tra Peninsula at a glance

Son Tra lies northeast of the city centre, joined to the mainland at Man Thai – Tho Quang. Most of it is protected forest run by the Son Tra Peninsula and Da Nang Beaches Management Board. The highest point is around Ban Co peak, roughly 700 m above sea level (sources give 693 – 700 m).

Think of it in two layers: the coastal Hoang Sa road on the south side (easy, leading to Linh Ung Pagoda and small beaches) and the mountain sightseeing routes, which have checkpoints, opening hours and vehicle rules.

8 things to see on Son Tra

1. Linh Ung Pagoda and the Lady Buddha

Son Tra's largest pagoda, built from 2004 and completed in 2010 on Hoang Sa road. Its Lady Buddha (Quan The Am) statue stands 67 m tall on a lotus base about 35 m wide, facing the sea. Entry is free; opening hours vary by source, commonly around 6:00 – 18:00 (indicative). Tip: arrive before 8 am to beat the tour groups and the heat, and cover shoulders and knees for the main hall.

2. The coastal Hoang Sa road

The easiest and most scenic stretch of the peninsula: forest on one side, Da Nang Bay on the other. It suits newer riders and families in a car. Tip: only stop where the shoulder is wide, never on a bend.

3. Ban Co peak

The “rooftop of Da Nang”, at about 700 m. A statue of an immortal sits pondering a stone chessboard, linked to the legend of two immortals playing chess — one lost after being distracted by fairies bathing at Tien Sa beach. On a clear day you can see the whole city, the bay and the Hai Van Pass. Tip: if drizzle and cloud cover the summit, you will see only white — change your day.

4. The thousand-year banyan

Recognised as a Vietnam Heritage Tree on 8 June 2014. Its age is quoted as anything from over 800 to nearly 1,000 years; it is about 22 m tall, with a vast cluster of trunks and aerial roots. Under the current visitor plan, the path from the Bai Bac junction to the tree is walking only. Tip: wear shoes with grip and do not climb the roots.

5. Bai Bac

The north-side beach at the end of the one-way descent from Ban Co, with clear water and a wilder feel than My Khe. Tip: there are no lifeguards like on the city beaches — swim only when the sea is calm and never alone.

6. Bai But and the small Hoang Sa beaches

Bai But lies below Linh Ung Pagoda; further along Hoang Sa road are small coves such as Bai Rang and Bai Nam, with rocks and clear water. The calm southern waters are also visited by SUP and snorkelling tours. Tip: bring water shoes — the seabed is rocky.

7. The forest and douc-watching spots

The forest edge along the mountain roads is where red-shanked doucs are most often seen in the early morning. Drive slowly and scan the high canopy — see the langur section below.

8. Man Thai at the foot of the mountain

An old fishing village with a quiet beach, basket boats near the shore and plenty of seafood restaurants — the natural end to a Son Tra loop. Tip: come at sunrise to watch the boats come in.

Note: Mui Nghe, Huc Lo (Ghenh Bang) and Bai Da Den are popular on social media but unauthorised trekking there is banned — see the trekking section.

Getting there and vehicle rules

Under the visitor plan published in August 2026, the mountain routes are open 7:30 am – 6:30 pm (March – September) and 7:30 am – 5:30 pm (October – February). The three main routes:

  • Yet Kieu – Ban Co peak – Bai Bac: one-way; closed to bicycles, automatic scooters and vehicles over 24 seats. Small cars and manual or semi-automatic motorbikes are allowed.
  • Tien Sa – Suoi Om – Ban Co peak: bicycles, motorbikes (not scooters) and walkers; closed for storm repairs from August 2026 — ask whether it has reopened.
  • Bai Bac junction – heritage banyan: walking, sightseeing and picnics only.

Trekking and watching red-shanked doucs responsibly

The red-shanked douc has five-coloured fur, maroon “stockings” and an orange-gold face. Population estimates for Son Tra vary by survey: about 1,300 in a 2017 census, with a newer survey suggesting more than 2,000.

  • Best time: early morning and late afternoon, along the forest edge on the mountain roads. The dry months (roughly February – August) are easier.
  • Do not feed them, throw things or shout. Keep your distance, use a zoom lens and no flash.
  • Never stop in the middle of the road for photos; pull over safely.

Trekking: unauthorised treks down to Huc Lo, Mui Nghe and Bai Da Den are strictly banned because of rough terrain and big waves, and many hikers have had to be rescued. Entering the special-use forest for trekking without permission is against the rules, so avoid informal trekking tours. The board also advises postponing hikes during heavy rain or thunderstorms. Beaches and water activities

In the calm-sea season (roughly April – August), the water around Son Tra is clear and has coral reefs. Paddle out for sunrise, take a boat trip to snorkel, or simply swim at a small cove. See our guides to stand-up paddleboarding in Da Nang and snorkelling and coral in Da Nang. Never stand on coral or take coral and shells home.

Where to eat: Man Thai seafood

There is almost nothing to buy on the mountain, so bring water and snacks. Back at the bottom, the Man Thai – Tho Quang area has plenty of casual seafood places near the beach. Ordering tips: ask the price per kilo, watch the weighing before it is cooked, and order what is in season (snails, squid, fish). Early risers can have bún chả cá (fishcake noodle soup) before heading back.

Safety on Son Tra

  • Steep roads, tight bends: descend in a low gear and use engine braking; constant braking overheats brakes until they fade.
  • Rain: wet roads are slippery and landslides or fallen trees are possible — do not go up during heavy rain or right after a storm.
  • Check fuel, brakes and tyres first; there is no petrol station on the mountain.
  • Do not ride at night: routes are closed outside visiting hours, roads are dark and wildlife crosses.
  • Wear a helmet, carry a raincoat, water and sunscreen, and save your hotel's number.
  • No fires, no litter, no picking plants, no catching animals.

FAQ

Can I ride a scooter on Son Tra?

Partly. Hoang Sa road to Linh Ung Pagoda is fine on a scooter. The Yet Kieu – Ban Co – Bai Bac route is closed to automatic scooters and bicycles; only manual or semi-automatic motorbikes and vehicles up to 24 seats. Ride yourself only if you are used to mountain descents.

What are the opening hours?

Under the 2026 visitor plan: 7:30 am – 6:30 pm from March to September and 7:30 am – 5:30 pm from October to February. Routes may close at short notice in the rainy season.

How tall is the Lady Buddha in Da Nang?

67 m, on a lotus base about 35 m wide. Linh Ung Pagoda is free to enter.

How high is Ban Co peak?

About 700 m (sources give 693 – 700 m), the highest point of the peninsula.

When am I most likely to see red-shanked doucs?

Early morning and late afternoon, especially in the dry months from about February to August. Never feed them and keep your distance.

Is November or December a good time for Son Tra?

You can visit Linh Ung and Man Thai on dry days, but the mountain routes often close because of heavy rain and landslides. In 2026, visits were suspended from 8 October and from the afternoon of 10 November. Check the status before you go.
